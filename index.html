Physics, interpretation & limitations

Each yaw slider rotates one complete layer about its center around the vertical (+y) axis. Crystal positions, orientations, ray attenuation and reconstruction response all rotate. Overlapping crystal configurations are rejected. The low-energy threshold applies to both constrained hit energies. Gamma paths show expected accepted routes for the source, independent of acquisition noise; they are not individual simulated events.

The source emits one 1 MeV gamma per decay. 1 µCi = 37,000 decays/s. Expected emitted photons equal activity × measurement time; expected accepted counts multiply that by the geometry-dependent two-hit efficiency. The observed acquisition is a Poisson sample. Activity is constant over the measurement; no half-life or dead time is modeled.

The room, table and detector supports are visual context only: they do not attenuate or scatter gamma rays. Detector geometry remains in centimeters; the room is in meters. The detector-view image is a live render from the camera midpoint, with every heat-map sample registered by viewing direction. This is a direction overlay, not a reconstruction of activity on the walls. A real photo would require camera calibration and pose registration.

The map covers a sphere at the selected radius, measured from the camera midpoint. Every pixel subtends the same solid angle. Range is assumed, not reconstructed. Click the map to move the source. Each geometry or acquisition change starts a fresh reconstruction.

Both interaction orders are summed for every A–B crystal pair; the algorithm sees crystal IDs and deposited energy, not the true order or an exact interaction position. A and B name physical layers; either can scatter or absorb.

The default generator samples 32,768 pairs of interaction positions uniformly inside crystal volumes, then weights them by Klein–Nishina scattering, inverse-square dilution, direct absorption and attenuation along exact ray paths through the crystals. This includes inter-crystal shadowing. It generates the restricted single-Compton/direct-absorption channel by importance sampling, without tracking billions of missed photons.

Reconstruction uses an approximate mean-chord response and first-order propagation of unknown crystal coordinates. Choose “Match reconstruction model” for a controlled consistency check. The volume generator independently integrates geometry and transport, but shares material coefficients and energy response; this is not validation against Geant4 or measured data.

Total attenuation comes from NIST La/Br mass mixing. The direct photoelectric proxy is total attenuation minus free-electron Compton and includes coherent scattering. Gaussian energy resolution starts at 2.6% FWHM at 662 keV with √E scaling. Two energy measurements are constrained to a known 1 MeV sum; this is not an independently smeared two-energy photopeak gate.

Binned Poisson MLEM includes directional sensitivity normalization. The map’s sensitivity view shows the approximate reconstruction response, not a full-sky volume simulation. The 72 × 36 equal-area grid and 10 keV bins limit apparent sharpness. A narrow spot is not a confidence region.

Gamma energy: 50–3000 keV. The absorption proxy includes non-Compton attenuation; above pair-production threshold it can overestimate full-energy absorption. Pair-production and escape events are not explicitly simulated.

Selection: one crystal in each layer, the selected low-energy threshold in both hits, full energy assumed at the selected gamma-ray energy. Excludes same-layer pairs, multiple scatters, housing, Doppler broadening, electron and fluorescence escape, intrinsic background, random coincidences and dead time. The mean-chord reconstruction is especially approximate at close spacing or grazing incidence. Efficiency estimates cover only the toy channel, not calibrated photopeak efficiency.

Longer separation can improve axis determination while reducing the solid angle for coincidences. Near-forward scatters can fall below the selected threshold; backscatters and order ambiguity can dominate. Spreading the crystals changes angular coverage as well as efficiency, so the trade-off need not be monotonic.

Singles provide no individual Compton cone. They can support spectroscopy or a calibrated directional count-rate likelihood, which is not included here.
